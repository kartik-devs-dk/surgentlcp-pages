Life care plan vs. medical cost projection.

Two instruments, one job: putting a defensible number on future medical care. Here's how to pick the right one for your case — from a firm that prepares both.

The one-paragraph answer

A life care plan is the comprehensive instrument: evaluation-based, every category of lifetime care, litigation-grade sourcing, built to survive expert deposition and trial. A medical cost projection is the abbreviated one: records-only, focused on future medical costs, delivered in days rather than weeks at a fraction of the fee — built for demand letters, mediation, and early resolution.

Neither is "better." Catastrophic, contested cases need the plan. Earlier, smaller, or deadline-driven matters are often best served by the projection — and over-buying wastes both fee and time.

The decision framework

Four questions settle it:

1
How severe is the injury?

Catastrophic or permanent multi-system injury → plan. Finite treatment horizon → projection.

2
Will future damages be fought?

If the number faces an opposing expert at deposition, it needs the full plan behind it. If it needs to be credible in a demand or mediation, the projection carries.

3
What's the procedural posture?

Pre-suit or early → projection now, escalate later if needed. Past expert-disclosure planning → the plan, engaged with 4–6 weeks of runway.

4
Does the budget match the case value?

A mid-five-figure case rarely justifies a five-figure expert spend. Match the instrument to the damages at stake.

The escalation path matters more than the initial choice: a physician-signed projection by the expert who would author the full plan converts cleanly if the case grows teeth. Template projections from volume vendors don't — which is the hidden cost of the cheapest option.
